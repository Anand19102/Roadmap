# ⏳ SPACED REVISION SYSTEM

> **Purpose:** Build long-term retention without repeatedly studying everything from scratch.

---

# 1. BASIC PRINCIPLE

New information is reviewed at increasing intervals.

The exact intervals are flexible.

The system is:

```text
Learn
↓
Short review
↓
Later review
↓
Longer review
↓
Maintenance
```

---

# 2. DEFAULT REVISION WINDOWS

For important concepts, use approximately:

```text
Day 0  → Learn
Day 1  → Quick recall
Day 3  → Recall + small practice
Day 7  → Weekly revision
Day 14 → Revisit
Day 30 → Monthly revision
Day 60+ → Maintenance
```

These are **guidelines**, not rigid deadlines.

---

# 3. FAILURE-BASED SCHEDULING

If you completely forget a topic:

Move it back to a shorter interval.

Example:

```text
Day 30 review
↓
Forgot most of it
↓
Review again tomorrow
↓
Review after 3 days
↓
Review after 7 days
```

---

# 4. MASTERY-BASED SCHEDULING

If you recall a concept instantly and can apply it:

Increase the interval.

Example:

```text
Day 7 → excellent
Day 14 → excellent
Day 30 → excellent
Day 60 → excellent
```

There is no need to repeatedly spend large amounts of time on it.

---

# 5. DSA SPACED REVISION

For DSA, use:

### Concept

Recall the pattern.

### Template

Write or mentally reconstruct the template.

### Problem

Re-solve one or more representative problems.

### Recognition

Look at a new problem and identify the likely pattern.

---

# 6. JAVA SPACED REVISION

Use:

* syntax recall
* small coding exercises
* OOP explanations
* collection comparisons
* implementation exercises

---

# 7. CORE CS SPACED REVISION

Use:

* active recall
* short-answer questions
* diagrams
* interview explanations
* comparisons

Example:

> Process vs Thread

Rather than rereading a chapter, explain the difference yourself.

---

# 8. SQL SPACED REVISION

Use:

* query reconstruction
* query writing
* query debugging
* increasingly complex questions

---

# 9. REVISION PRIORITY

The more important and difficult a topic is, the more frequently it should return.

Factors:

```text
Importance
+
Difficulty
+
Frequency of mistakes
+
Interview relevance
=
Revision priority
```

---

# 10. WEEKEND BUFFER

Saturday/Sunday can absorb:

* missed revisions
* unfinished tasks
* weak topics
* accumulated practice

This prevents one bad weekday from destabilizing the entire roadmap.

---

# 11. DO NOT LET REVISION BECOME A SECOND CURRICULUM

Revision must remain proportionate.

The objective is not:

> "Spend half the week revising."

Instead:

> "Keep previous learning alive while continuing forward."

---

# 12. FINAL PRINCIPLE

> **The roadmap moves forward, while revision continuously pulls important knowledge back into active memory.**
