# 🧠 KEEP IN MIND — READ ME FIRST

> **Purpose:** This folder contains the permanent rules, systems, protocols and principles that govern the entire MEC Career Roadmap.

---

# 1. What is this folder?

`02_KEEP_IN_MIND` is **not a syllabus**.

It is the **operating manual** for the preparation journey.

The Master Roadmap tells me:

> **WHAT I will learn, WHEN I will learn it, and WHY it is being placed there.**

The Current Execution Plan tells me:

> **WHAT I actually need to do today/this week.**

This folder tells me:

> **HOW I am supposed to learn, practice, revise, build, solve, track and recover.**

Therefore, I should not need to repeatedly reread the entire planning conversation to remember how the system works.

# 3. The fundamental principle

## The roadmap is a system, not a checklist.

The goal is **not**:

> Finish as many videos as possible.

The goal is:

> Become employable and interview-ready through progressively deeper understanding, deliberate practice, projects and revision.

Therefore:

**Completion ≠ mastery.**

Watching a course video does not mean the topic is learned.

Solving one guided problem does not mean the pattern is mastered.

Building a tutorial project does not mean I can build independently.

Reading interview questions does not mean I can explain the concepts.

# 7. Learning depth matters

Every meaningful topic receives a mastery classification.

The five levels are defined in:

`01_MASTERY_CLASSIFICATION_SYSTEM.md`

Never assume that every topic deserves equal time.

Some topics should be mastered deeply.

Some should be understood strongly.

# 8. The anti-burnout principle

The roadmap must be ambitious **without being stupidly aggressive**.

I am a college student with:

* Monday–Friday classes
* long college days
* gym commitments
* exams
* assignments
* college events
* varying daily energy

Therefore:

> **The roadmap must be sustainable enough to survive an entire academic year.**

The first stage should establish consistency.

The workload can progressively increase.

The objective is not to destroy myself during Week 1 and abandon the system during Week 2.

---

# 9. Daily consistency > perfect daily schedules

Some days will naturally have:

* more study time
* less study time
* gym
* no gym
* assignments
* exams
* college events

That is acceptable.

The system therefore uses:

```text
DAILY EXECUTION
       +
WEEKLY COMPLETION TARGET
```

The weekly target is the primary safety net.

If Tuesday is highly productive and Thursday is busy, the week can still succeed.

---

# 10. Weekend catch-up

Saturday and Sunday are not automatically "free days".

They provide flexibility.

If I fall behind during the week:

> Weekend = recovery/catch-up opportunity.

If I am already on schedule:

> Weekend = reinforcement, revision, project work, deeper practice or lighter workload.

The objective is:

> **Do not allow a bad day to become a bad week.**

---

# 11. Never blindly follow the schedule

The roadmap is carefully designed, but reality can change.

Examples:

* college workload increases
* exams approach
* internship opportunity appears
* placement notification changes
* a resource becomes obsolete
* a topic takes longer than expected
* a topic is already mastered
* a prerequisite turns out to be weak

The system can therefore adapt.

But adaptation should happen **strategically**, not emotionally.

---

# 12. The golden rule

Whenever uncertain, ask:

> **"What produces the highest long-term career ROI from the time I have available right now?"**

That question protects the roadmap from:

* shiny-object syndrome
* endless courses
* unnecessary certifications
* premature technologies
* random YouTube learning
* excessive tooling
* tutorial addiction
